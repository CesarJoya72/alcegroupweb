What Alce is

Great Companies Are Not Built to Be Sold.

And yet, a moment comes when almost every owner asks the same question: who is going to run this ten years from now.

The usual options are more alike than they seem. A fund buys to sell within four or five years, and everything it does in the meantime is geared towards that sale. A competitor buys to absorb: the brand, the team and the way of doing things last only as long as the integration.

Alce Is Something Else

Alce is not a fund. It is a business group that buys companies and keeps them: no deadlines, no one waiting for an exit, no integration plans. The cash the companies generate is reinvested in buying more, and so on year after year.

It is a model that has worked for decades in northern Europe and the United States. In Spain and Portugal, no established buyer does this yet. We want to be among the first.

How We Work

We do not get involved in the day-to-day. Decisions are made at the company, which is where the information is. We bring what a company on its own does not usually have: financing to grow, help with key hires and the experience of the other companies in the group.

We do not buy at any price. We buy few companies, well chosen, at sensible prices and without excessive debt. If a deal does not make sense, we would rather wait.

We do not change the price at the last minute. The price we agree is the price we pay. Pre-closing reviews are there to confirm, not to cut.

We do not sell. What the companies earn is reinvested in growing them and in buying others. That is how a group that lasts decades is built.

Criteria

The Companies We Buy

We buy companies in Spain and Portugal that, in general, meet these conditions. If yours sounds familiar, let's talk.

  1. Size

    Companies earning between one and four million euros a year in EBITDA.

  2. Profitability

    Double-digit margins and predictable cash generation, without needing heavy investment every year just to keep going.

  3. Position

    Leaders in their niche, with a solid position in the value chain. You do not have to be big: you have to be the best at something specific.

  4. Customers

    B2B business, with recurring revenue and a diversified customer base.

  5. Team

    We prefer the owner to stay at the helm. When that is not possible, an orderly transition plan works too.

  6. Sector

    We care about the business, not whether the sector is in fashion. We prefer light manufacturing, value-added distribution and business services, but if the business is good and we understand it, it fits.

For owners

What Changes and What Does Not

Selling is the biggest decision an owner makes about their company, and it is almost never just a matter of price. These are the things an owner always asks us, answered up front.

The Owner Stays at the Helm

Our preferred scenario is that the owner keeps running the company. If at some point they want to step back, we prepare it together and with as much time ahead as it takes: the natural path is for their second line to take over. Bringing in someone from outside is the last resort, not the first.

The Owner Stays a Partner

We buy the majority of the equity and we ask the owner to keep a meaningful stake; in most cases, around a quarter. It is not a formality: we want them to keep gaining from what is built from here on. If they need to sell one hundred per cent, we can talk about it, but that is the exception.

The Day-to-Day Does Not Change

We will not get involved in operations. We do not buy to cut back or to dismantle what works; we buy precisely because it works. Their people keep doing their jobs, with the same people as always.

The Name Stays

No integration, no rebranding, no becoming a division of something else. The company stays the company.

The Owner Secures Their Wealth

Almost everything the owner has built sits inside the company. With the deal they secure a significant part of it and no longer have everything in one place, without giving up what comes next.
